# How to Choose Running Shoes for Beginners

Choosing your first running shoes is easier than it looks. The right pair fits well, feels comfortable from the first step and suits the surfaces you run on.

## Start with fit

Leave about a thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the shoe. Try pairs on in the afternoon, when feet are slightly larger, and wear the socks you train in. A good fit matters more than any feature.

## Cushioning and drop

Cushioning is a matter of comfort. Beginners often like a moderate amount, which protects the legs while they adapt to the impact. The drop is the height difference between heel and toe; most new runners do well with a mid drop of 6 to 10 millimetres.

## Road or trail

Road shoes are lighter and smoother underfoot. Trail models have deeper grip and tougher uppers for mud and rocks. If you use both surfaces, start with a road pair and add trail shoes later.

## Where to buy

A specialist shop can watch your stride and suggest a model that matches it. Many let you test a pair on a treadmill, and some offer a trial period so you can return shoes that don't feel right.
